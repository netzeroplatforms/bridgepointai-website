What you are looking at

A companion note to the draft site, for Sam and Tracy · updated 9 August 2026

The draft is now sixteen pages, and it works as a site rather than a set of pages. Since the first note on 6 August the work has been about coherence: one capability argued in one place, one navigation and one footer everywhere, and something on every page that earns its keep. This note covers what changed, what is deliberately unfinished, and the three things we need a decision from you on.

Still not live and still cannot be found. It sits on BridgePointAI's site, marked noindex, blocked in robots.txt, with a bar across the bottom of every page stating it is a concept build. There is no link anywhere on the draft that leads back to hhglobalholdings.com, so nothing will bounce you out of it mid-read.

Why we did this at all

The site was not broken. It reads well, the argument is sound, and it is bringing enquiries in. This work started because of what is happening around it, not because of anything wrong with it.

Three things have changed since it was written. Lab 24 and EviDD are launching, and neither existed when the site was built. LabArchitect has moved from a capability to something you talk about extensively, which means prospects arrive already half-briefed and expecting depth. And the site is getting bites, so the people landing on it are no longer browsing. They are deciding.

That changes what the site has to do. A page that satisfies a casual reader is not the same as a page that holds a serious prospect who has already had the conversation and wants to know whether the substance is there. The job now is confidence at the point of landing: someone arrives, and within a screen or two they understand who HH is, what it can actually do, and that the claims will survive scrutiny.

The other half of it is that understanding HH should be enjoyable. The science is genuinely interesting and the argument is a good one. A prospect should want to keep scrolling because they are learning something, not because they are hunting for a capability list. That is why every moving thing on the draft explains something rather than decorating it, and why the capability pages now carry the argument in one place instead of the reader meeting it twice.

And underneath all of it, the positioning. HH is not competing with the industrial CROs and should not pretend to. It is filling a gap they do not serve, with its own laboratory, its own named scientists and its own furrow to plough. The site should read like an organisation that knows exactly which gap it fills, rather than a smaller version of somebody else.

The rule we applied to the whole build

Each capability is argued once, on its own page. The home page introduces it in a short paragraph and routes you there.

That was not true a week ago. The home page carried the clean room entry sequence in full and the clean room page carried it. It carried the assessor's checklist in full and the Competence Assurance page carried it. Reading top to bottom, you met the same argument twice, in the same words, with the same animation. The home page is now shorter and each capability page is the only place its case is made.

What was deliberately not done

No new claims. Unchanged from the first note, and worth restating now there is more on the draft. Nothing asserts an accreditation, certification or capability that is not already claimed on the live site. The maturity track states the status each programme holds, and those statuses are taken from the labels already on your own case study pages, not invented.

No AI-generated photography. Every photograph is yours. The only generated imagery is abstract texture, which makes no factual claim.

No worked example on the Evidence Synthesis page. The obvious one is under NDA and is not named anywhere. The page argues from the general case instead, which costs it its strongest illustration. If that changes, it is the single biggest improvement available to that page.

Your long-form copy on Company Profile and Scientific Practice. Both are substantial on the live site and were left close to as they are, deliberately. Case Studies and Innovation & IP now have diagrams, but the copy underneath them is still yours and still short, at 175 and 296 words. Whether that needs writing is a question for you, not a decision we should take.

Accessibility and restraint

  • Every animation stops for anyone whose system requests reduced motion.
  • Nothing is hidden by CSS waiting to be revealed. The concealing is applied by script, so if the script never runs, everything is simply visible. Each reveal also has a timed fallback. There is no state in which a visitor sees a blank panel.
  • All diagrams carry text descriptions for screen readers.
  • No horizontal overflow at any width from 390px to 1440px.

What we need from you

  1. The pricing. The Evidence Synthesis page shows from £15k for a rapid review, £20–60k for a full systematic review, and up to £90k for regulatory-grade work. Those bands are reconciled from three different figures in the business plan, and the plan itself notes that pricing is subject to your confirmation of what HH will stand behind. It is also the only price anywhere on the site. Confirm the numbers, or tell us to take them off.
  2. Controlled Environments. The page exists and reads well, but its argument about provenance being lost at handover now sits on the Lab 24 page where it lands better. The page is currently in the build with nothing linking to it. Bring it back into the menu, or retire it.
  3. Case Studies and Innovation & IP. Both are thin. Do you want them written out, and if so, is there work we have not been told about that should be on them?

And the question from the first note still stands, because it is the one that matters: does this feel like HH, or like someone else's site wearing your colours?
