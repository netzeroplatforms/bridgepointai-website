Evidence Synthesis
Systematic review and critical appraisal

A Systematic Review Is Not a Summary
of What Has Been Published.

It is a judgement about what can be relied on. Screening is the part machines now do well. Deciding whether a study can carry the weight being placed on it is not, and that judgement is the only part that was ever worth paying for.

From retrieval to reliance

Two Thousand Records In.
Eleven Studies Out.

The number that matters is not how many papers were found. It is how many survived appraisal, and on what grounds the rest were set aside. That is the part of the process a reader can audit, and the part a search tool cannot perform.

Illustrative flow. Reported to PRISMA, appraised with the instrument that fits the study type, QUADAS-2 for diagnostic accuracy rather than a tool designed for trials.

Where reviews fail, and it is rarely arithmetic

The Weakness Is Almost Never
That Too Few Papers Were Found.

A review can be exhaustive in its search and still reach a conclusion the evidence does not support. The failures cluster in a small number of places, and every one of them is a judgement about study design rather than a gap in retrieval.

Reference
A reference standard that quietly included the test being evaluated, so the study is in part measuring itself and the accuracy figure flatters accordingly.
Design
A two gate design that recruits obvious cases and healthy controls separately, producing a separation the test will not reproduce in the population it is sold into.
Outcome
Outcomes reported that are not the outcomes the protocol specified, and sensitivity quoted with no statement of the population it was measured in.
What we do

Listing Papers Is Not
the Same as Appraising Them.

A literature section that is a list of papers with a paragraph under each, presented as though listing were the same as appraising, will not survive a regulatory review. Under the EU medical device and IVD regulations the literature behind a clinical or performance evaluation must be systematic in the strict sense.

Protocol written before results are looked at

Registered where registration applies, with the question, the inclusion criteria and the outcomes fixed in advance rather than settled once the data is in view.

Documented, reproducible search

Databases, registers, search strings and dates recorded so a third party can run the same search and arrive at the same starting set.

Flow diagram accounting for every exclusion

Reported to PRISMA, so the path from records identified to studies synthesised is auditable at each step rather than asserted at the end.

Critical appraisal against a fitted instrument

QUADAS-2 for diagnostic accuracy, the appropriate risk of bias tool for trials and observational designs. The instrument follows the study type, not house habit.

Synthesis that states its own confidence

What the body of evidence supports, how strongly, and where it does not reach. Including, where it is the honest answer, that the question remains open.

Regulatory-grade documentation

Written to carry the clinical or performance evaluation behind an MDR or IVDR submission, rather than requiring rework once it reaches a notified body.

The difference

Further Research Is Needed.
We Have a Laboratory.

Almost every systematic review ends in some version of that sentence, and for almost everyone producing them a document is the only thing they can make. Universities, medical writers and synthesis consultancies stop there because stopping there is the limit of what they own.

When a review identifies the study that is missing, we can design it, run it and produce the data. The synthesis and the means of closing the gap it exposes are the same proposition, delivered by the same organisation, with the appraisal standard carried through into how the new work is specified.
