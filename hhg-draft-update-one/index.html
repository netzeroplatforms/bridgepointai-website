Evidence synthesis

A systematic review is not a summary
of what has been published.

It is a judgement about what can be relied on. Screening is the part machines now do well. Deciding whether a study can carry the weight being placed on it is not, and that judgement is the only part that was ever worth paying for.

Reviews rarely fail for finding too few papers. They fail on a reference standard that quietly included the test being evaluated, a two gate design, outcomes that were not the ones the protocol specified. And almost every review ends in some version of further research is needed. We have a laboratory, so when a review identifies the study that is missing, we can design it, run it and produce the data.

Competence assurance

Attendance is not
competence.

ISO 17025 does not ask whether somebody attended a course. It asks whether a named individual is competent and authorised for a specific activity, and whether that competence continues to be monitored. Assessors have seen a great many attendance certificates.

The gap, and why it surfaces late

Training is bought as attendance and recorded as attendance. The standard requires demonstrated competence, assessed against the activity and monitored over time. The two are not the same, and the difference tends to surface during an assessment rather than before one.

The finding is rarely that people were untrained. It is almost always that nobody could demonstrate otherwise.

We build the competence record alongside the training, so that what is left behind is evidence rather than a certificate.
